## Nesting and conflicting masks

Prashant had surveyed how comparable systems handle nesting or conflicting masks. Daniel asked for clarifying examples; the major one is inner versus outer null masking rules, and he thought out loud about an inner-to-outer order. Nevin supported disabling the behavior in the spec for now: read restrictions are a contract between the catalog or policy engine and the client, catalogs, policy engines and end users should carry more responsibility for sensible outcomes, and the restriction can be relaxed later; he wanted a concrete proposal with scope and effort before considering an evaluation order. Laurent supported disabling it in the spec and delegating conflicts to the catalog or authorizer, Corey supported the direction but warned to be careful about how rules are relaxed later, and Sung favored being restrictive for now with future discussion. Prashant asked Daniel for a concrete ask: a deep dive on what policy engines do, or an order of operations that is safe and consistent.

**Open questions:**
- Disallow nested restrictions (safe and simple, possibly a client exception on an invalid restriction set), or define an execution order that is generally safe?
- Is there a definable, bottom-up execution order, like an expression tree, that always works across policy engines?
- Which responsibilities belong to the catalog and policy engine versus the client or execution engine?
